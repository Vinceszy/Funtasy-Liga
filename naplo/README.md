# Measurement archive

Raw logs of one-off observations. The files stay because the raw data in them
may still be useful later (e.g. if the FPL changes its behaviour mid-season,
there is something to compare against). The conclusions are in the main README
(section 3/b. The close, and in section 4 the subsections "Automatic
substitutions at the round's close (PL)", "Who is still on the pitch (PL)" and
"The three states of bonus points (PL)").

Every probe runs from **one** workflow (`.github/workflows/naplo-meres.yml`):
the development environment's network blocks every external source, so the
probes run from Actions. Parameters are passed in the `environment` input (one
`NAME=value` per line).

The **filled colour table** of each round is here too (`nb1-colour-<round>.json`,
`pl-colour-<round>.json`): one claim per row under `claims`, in the form
`{player, minute, tag, sentence, anchor, match}` (PL rows about a footballer also
carry `club`, and `source_name` where the names differ; the fields are described
in `colour-taxonomy.md`). The pieces are written from this table, not from the
text of the reports — so every sentence can be traced back, and the anchor
decides whether it may be stated (as fact) or is left out; a piece never cites
a source, only a manager's own words stand as a quotation.

The season's background facts supplied by the editors (who drafted whom, who
took Kele's column to heart) are kept per league in `nb1-background.json` and
`pl-background.json`, under `facts`: `{manager|team, fact, from_round, used_in}`.
In the PL by team name, never by the real name.

Every piece starts with one command: `python3 naplo/article-prep.py <nb1|pl>
<round> <summary|preview|column>` - it prints the rules, the spent material and the
round's data. The method is in one place: `round-pipeline.md`.

Nemzethy Sport's **rule documents** are here too; they are not measurements:
`round-pipeline.md` (the method: what runs first, where the data comes from,
in what order), `summary-voice.md` (how the summary and the preview sound),
`colour-taxonomy.md` (which fact counts as colour, and which source it may come
from), and `kele-janek.md` (the weekly column's voice and what it may not do —
it belongs not to a fixture but to the round).

| `nb1-round-state.txt` + `nb1-round-state-probe.py` | what was happening with round 9, about which the collector logged for weeks only that "the squads are not available yet (before the market closes)" | the inference was TRUE: the round lasted three weeks (because of the international break) and its market only closed at the end. The round object carries `closed_transfers_at` and `is_transfers_closed` - from these it is **known in advance** when the squad first becomes available, but the collector does not read them, only the 403. The probe also showed that the collector generalises from a single manager: it tries the first one and takes the answer as valid for all eight (here they agreed) |
| `article-prep.py` | not a probe: the first step of every piece | prints the rule files for the kind of piece in full, the published pieces the new one may not repeat, and the data - for a preview the season mirror of the two managers (margins, round ranks, form, table, previous meeting; for the NB1 also what the last squads shared and whether the real fixtures are recorded), for a summary the round harvest. It refuses a summary of an open round and a preview of a round that is not the coming one |
| `nb1-fixtures-10.txt` | who plays whom in NB I round 10 (for the previews) | the fantasy API does not serve future matches (every match endpoint 404, the squad response 403 until the market closes), so the real programme was taken from two independent web sources; all six pairings agree, the kick-off times do not, so they are not used |
| `nb1-fixtures-11.txt` | who plays whom in NB I round 11 (for the previews) | two independent schedule pages, read by `fixtures-probe.py`, agree in all six pairings |
| `fixtures-probe.txt` + `fixtures-probe.py` | the published NB I programme of one round (`PROBE_URLS`, `PROBE_ROUND`), from schedule pages | the development network reaches none of these sites, and the web fetcher cannot resolve them either, so the programme pages are read from Actions; the page text stays in the run log, the record of each round is `nb1-fixtures-<round>.txt` |
| `fpl-state.txt` + `fpl-state-watcher.py` | the FPL round flags, every quarter of an hour | there is no daily close; the bonus becomes final at the round's lockdown (2026-08-25, 08:03–08:23 UTC, in two steps) |
| `fpl-minutes.txt` + `fpl-minutes-probe.py` | player minutes during a live match, every 2 minutes | the fixtures' `minutes` is always 0; the match clock is the maximum of the players' minutes; a substituted player's minutes freeze; ~3 minutes of data delay |
| `fpl-subs.txt` + `fpl-subs-probe.py` | the automatic substitutions at the end of the round | the FPL rewrites the pick's `position` and also gives a separate `subs` list; the signal of the close is `current_event_finished` |
| `proxy-probe.txt` + `proxy-probe.py` | 9 CORS proxies + the two APIs directly, with an Origin header (2026-08-27, during a live outage) | the MLSZ and FPL responses carry no ACAO (a direct browser request will never work); corsproxy.io switched to 401, allorigins is overloaded; `proxy.cors.sh` works for both APIs — the route list in the `funtasy.js` fetcher is set accordingly |
| `mlsz-data.txt` + `mlsz-data-probe.py` | what the MLSZ API gives for the NB1 | the match object DOES carry the result; there is NO "was he a starter" data; the rows of the bulk minutes query cannot be tied to a player |
| `mlsz-live-match.txt` + `mlsz-live-match-probe.py` | whether the two clubs of a match can be had for a LIVE round (2026-08-30) | yes, but **only with an explicit** `games.home_team`/`games.away_team` include (+2.5 KB, without logos); there is no separate match endpoint (10 forms, all 404); from the owned players only 4 of 6 matches would come out, and home/away not at all |
| `ratings.txt` + `ratings-fetch.py` | what the readers said about the pieces (the Worker's `/ertekelesek` endpoint) | the count, the 1–4 distribution and the per-article breakdown go into the log; **the reasons show only in the run's log**, they do not go into the repository |
| `mlsz-player-list.txt` + `mlsz-player-list-probe.py` | whether the MLSZ has a **future schedule** (for the profile's next opponents), and how exact a player's base points are | there is no standalone match endpoint (~25 forms, all 404); a player's base points are **always a multiple of 0.25** (238 rounds, without exception), so the rounding of the bench halving can be computed back |
| `draft-preview-harvest.py` | the **preview layer** of the next Draft round, from our own data | it does not look at what happened but at what affects next week's result: how often the machine had to step in, how many points stayed out on the bench, how much the squad moves brought, how much ballast is in the line-up, and how much the team depends on a single player |
| `mlsz-price-history.txt` + `mlsz-price-history-probe.py` | whether the player sheet gives a price per round, and which field the round comes from | the list body gives only the current price, and our own price log only accumulates from its own start; the row's `round_id` is the round's identifier (75 + 2 × round), not the row's `id` — mixing the two up would silently put the prices in the wrong round |
| `kele-voice-analysis.py` | **counts**, offline, in how many of the author's own pieces each device of the voice appears, from the texts under `tartalek/kele/`. His own pieces: the pages titled „Kele János:" or matching his file-name patterns (`kele-janos`, `gegenpressing`, `velemenycikk`) and the three Substack essays saved by hand (`substack-*.txt`); the template formats (the publisher's podcast trailers, which he did not write, and the class registers, a player-by-player grading) are left out. Everything not counted as his own is the control. It also measures the six claims of the column's first description as densities against that control, and how often a sentence with a number carries a judgement | a device belongs to the author's voice if it appears in several pieces; the form of a single piece belongs to that piece. The script does not separate the sources: the Substack essays are counted in the same pool as the 24.hu pieces. On the current corpus (63 pages: 18 his own, 45 control) two devices are general - an interjection between dashes (10 of 18) and a verbless opening (8 of 18). The Substack essays' devices (self-correction in the second sentence, „Félreértés ne essék" (make no mistake), the three concessions, numbered theses) each come out `single`, 1 of 18, and every one of those hits is in a Substack essay - **not once** in a 24.hu football piece: they belong to the voice of his Substack essays, not of the football column. Against the control he uses about twice the density of numbers, half the superlatives and a third of the stated emotion |
| `kele-voice-probe.py` | the source of the imitated voice: walks the author's 24.hu author page (`KELE_PAGES` archive pages, default 6), downloads the linked articles (at most `KELE_ARTICLES`, default 40) and saves every page that carries the author's link as text under `tartalek/kele/` | the column imitates the voice of an existing columnist, and that has to be read, not guessed. The author page also carries the publisher's "latest news" strip, full of other people's articles: a bare dated-article-link pattern collects those too, and the probe then brings back weather forecasts and crime news while looking successful. So for every downloaded page the **byline** (the author's link `/author/kelejanos` on the page) decides, not where the link was found, and at the end the probe prints how many pages it dropped. The byline check keeps every page with his link, not only his own running text: of the 60 24.hu pages under `tartalek/kele/`, 34 are the publisher's podcast trailers and 5 are class registers - telling them apart is the job of `kele-voice-analysis.py`. The probe does not fetch Substack (its protection refuses every request from a data-centre address with 403, the feed and the archive API too): the three `substack-*.txt` essays were saved by hand. The text goes into the repository — a run log can no longer be read back after a few days |
| `transfer-harvest.py` | the NB1 transfer layer round by round: who changed how many players, what the squad was worth before and after, whether the starting shape and the armband changed, and what the players sold had scored the round before | the weekly limit is **3** transfers, unlimited in the winter and international breaks - and in the season so far exactly **one round** saw anyone go over three: round 9, after a 20-day gap. Round 8 also followed a break (12 days), but nobody used it. The break is measured from the **gap**, not from the round's length: for a running round the collector stores only the matches played, and a three-week round would look two days long |
| `armband-harvest.py` | the NB1 captaincy layer over several rounds: who put the armband on whom, when they changed their mind, and what it brought compared with the best player of their own squad | a single round's captain list says nothing; four consecutive rounds show whether someone decides or only clings on — in rounds 5–8, of twenty-four chances to reconsider, five were a change. The stored `week` is already doubled for the captain and already halved for a bench player, so it has to be divided back |
| `bench-harvest.py` | the Draft bench layer: the points left on the bench, the surplus a perfect line-up could have won, and the losses where the bench alone was more than the margin | the three numbers are not the same, and are easy to mix up: the 272 left on the bench is an upper bound (four players cannot all be put into the eleven), the real damage, computed with the pairing, is +209 |
| `timeline.py` | the round's events on ONE clock, fixture by fixture | the goal minute added to the kick-off time shows where a fixture turned, and what happened meanwhile on another pitch — in round 5 Isak's and Semenyo's goals fell in the same minute in two different matches |
| `colour-check.py` | checks a round's colour table — offline, against our own data | every `event` and `record` row finds its goal, assist, VAR decision or big chance in the structured block (`matches`) of the same file (only the kinds the block holds); NB1: an `event` goal row's scorer has a goal in the stored breakdown; PL: a row with a `club` is in somebody's squad exactly when its anchor is not `record`; every statement names its speaker, and every PL match has its kick-off time. Without it the measurement's result lives only in memory |
| `pl-textstream.py` | the Premier League's **own minute-by-minute commentary** for a round (pulselive), paired with our fixtures | this is the PL's prose source — FotMob gives only structured data; the text goes only into the run's log, not into the repository |
| `draft-round-harvest.py` | a Draft round's fantasy layer from our own data (line-up points, points left on the bench, zero scorers, weekly moves) — the PL pair of `round-colour-harvest.py` | no network needed; the Draft has no captain and no shared player, so what is at stake is the line-up and the waiver |
| `storage-probe.txt` + `storage-probe.py` | the Worker's "last known score" store, end to end | the stored response arrives ~35× faster than a fresh fetch; with unchanged data the timestamp is not rewritten |
| `fpl-profile.txt` + `fpl-profile-probe.py` | the shape of the FPL `detail` text (`BHA (A) 4-0`) | the score pair is always in **home–away** order, not from the player's point of view — without this every away match would show reversed |
| `match-event-sources.txt` + `match-event-sources.py`, `official-sources-probe.py` | whether minute-accurate match events are available without a key | the big data providers return 403 or forbid it in robots.txt; the official sites' text commentary remains |
| `event-source.txt` + `event-source-probe.py` | the same with key-based providers (ESPN, API-Football) | 403 without a key; even with a key, only for the big leagues |
| `report-source.txt` + `report-source-probe.py`, `nemzetisport-probe.py`, `nso-content-probe.py` | whether Hungarian match reports are available for the NB1 | Nemzeti Sport **allows** the article pages (its `robots.txt` forbids only the `/hirdetesek`, `/cikk-elonezet` and `/publicapi` paths), and `sitemapindex.xml` is the machine index of the articles — **this is the NB1's colour source**. A probe that reads the `robots.txt` with urllib's default agent reports a false ban: that agent gets 403 for the `robots.txt`, and `RobotFileParser` then takes everything as forbidden. The text of the reports goes only into the run's log, never into the repository |
| `pl-colour.txt` + `pl-colour-probe.py`, `pl-colour-harvest.py` (`pl-colour-4.json`), `round-colour-harvest.py` | where the colour of the PL rounds can come from without a key | FotMob allows it and gives it **structured** (goal minute and manner, VAR, missed chances, saves); the round's harvest is made from it |
| `draft-picks.txt` + `draft-picks-probe.py`, `draft-picks-harvest.py` | what we can learn about our own draft and the transactions | `choices` is available without a key (`draft_picks.json` is made from it); the waiver history is **tied to a login** (403), so it is not collected |
| `mlsz-double-match.txt` + `mlsz-double-match-probe.py` | what the MLSZ sends when a club has **two matches** in one fantasy round (a postponed match made up) — 2026-09-03, MLSZ round 7 | ETO's `current_round.games` list has **two elements**: `3F` (ETO–FTC, 3 Sept, the postponed round 3 match) and `7F` (ETO–HONVÉD, 6 Sept). So the MLSZ puts the match in the round **of the day it is made up**, but keeps the **original round number** on it. Every other club had 1 element. |
